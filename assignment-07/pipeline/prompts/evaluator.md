## The register these rules protect

The GDD's own high-concept line is the voice target:

> Choose an Ascendant operative. Survive one complete duel. Earn the spectacle.

Three short declaratives. No punch-up, no applause, no hedging. The game's first
design pillar is **Skill Creates Spectacle** — the reward for playing well is the
spectacle itself, which means copy that hands out praise has quietly changed what
the game rewards.

## How to grade

Return a score from 0.0 to 1.0.

- **1.0** — clipped, declarative, states what happened or what to do, and claims
  no reward the player did not earn.
- **0.7** — one mild slip: slightly warmer than the register wants, or one
  softening word.
- **0.4** — a clear violation: it congratulates, it hypes with exclamation
  marks, or it hedges an instruction into a suggestion.
- **0.0** — reads as a different game entirely. Cheerful mobile-game voice,
  stacked exclamation marks, or praise for a player who is losing.

Then write one sentence saying which rule was violated and how, or confirming
the line is clean. Name the rule by its id. Be specific about the words that
caused the deduction — a downstream refiner will read your sentence and rewrite
the line from it, so "too enthusiastic" is useless and "opens with 'Nice work!',
which congratulates the player (T1)" is actionable.

Judge the line as written. Do not rewrite it, do not suggest alternatives, and
do not comment on anything except tone.
